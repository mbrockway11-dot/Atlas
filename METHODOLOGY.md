# Atlas Methodology

## Core Premise

Traditional systems often describe entities using isolated variables.

Atlas proposes that persistent structural relationships contain more information than isolated attributes.

Identity is therefore modeled as a topology rather than a flat collection of features.

---

# Structural Identity

An identity consists of

- nodes
- relationships
- motifs
- structural roles
- archetypal organization

These combine into a persistent graph.

---

# Persistent Measurements

Atlas emphasizes measurements that remain stable under minor perturbations.

Examples include

- node persistence
- edge persistence
- motif recurrence
- structural entropy
- relational coherence

---

# Structural Roles

Nodes may occupy characteristic roles including

- hubs
- bridges
- leaves
- clusters
- articulation points

Role distributions become part of identity.

---

# Archetypal Layer

Higher-order abstractions are generated from recurring structural motifs.

Rather than assigning labels directly, Atlas derives archetypes from measurable graph organization.

---

# Interpretation Layer

Interpretation occurs only after measurements are computed.

This separation prevents symbolic descriptions from influencing quantitative calculations.

Pipeline

Measurement

↓

Aggregation

↓

Interpretation

Never

Interpretation

↓

Measurement

---

# Research Objectives

Atlas investigates whether persistent topology can provide useful representations across multiple domains including

- psychology
- biology
- economics
- organizations
- financial markets
- knowledge graphs
- social systems

---

# Scientific Principles

Atlas follows

- deterministic computation
- reproducibility
- modularity
- explainability
- measurable hypotheses

Every structural claim should be grounded in computable metrics.
