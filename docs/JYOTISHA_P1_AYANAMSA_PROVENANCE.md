# Jyotiṣa P1 — What Establishes the Lahiri Ayanāṃśa

**Status:** research notebook · **Type:** provenance investigation, not an
admission · **Purpose:** the cross-domain test — does the gematria
convention-vs-doctrine pattern recur, or differ?

Option 3, executed. The question is not "acquire BPHS" but the narrow one:
*what actually establishes the Lahiri ayanāṃśa* — a standard, a committee, a
text, or an emergent convention? The answer is a sharp **contrast** with
gematria, which is exactly the informative outcome.

## Finding, by evidentiary status

**Corroborated (directly inspected — Wikipedia used only as locator, naming the
underlying facts):**

- The Lahiri ayanāṃśa is **named after an individual** — the astronomer Nirmal
  Chandra Lahiri (1906–1980) — and is the most prevalent system in India.
- The **Calendar Reform Committee of India** was formed in **1952** under
  **Meghnad Saha**; its report was **published 1955**.
- **N.C. Lahiri was a member** of that committee.
- The committee's calendar was **adopted as an official Government of India
  standard in 1957** (Gazette of India, All India Radio, official
  communications).

**Inferred / provisional (not confirmed by the two inspected articles):**

- That the committee **specifically standardized the Lahiri (Chitrapakṣa)
  ayanāṃśa**. The inspected sources confirm the committee, Lahiri's membership,
  and the calendar's official status — but *not*, explicitly, the ayanāṃśa
  adoption. That link is widely stated and plausible (Lahiri sat on the
  committee; the official Rāṣṭrīya Pañcāṅga uses his ayanāṃśa) but requires the
  **committee report itself** or the **Rāṣṭrīya Pañcāṅga** (Positional Astronomy
  Centre, Govt of India) to move from inferred to corroborated.

**Provenance of these claims:** Wikipedia, used strictly as a **locator**
(inadmissible tier). The sources to inspect directly are the **Calendar Reform
Committee Report (1955)** and the **Rāṣṭrīya Pañcāṅga** — both plausibly
`normative_standard` (official governmental publications).

## The cross-domain contrast (the point)

The same framework question — *what establishes this construct?* — yields
**opposite-shaped answers** in two traditions:

| | Gematria value mapping | Lahiri ayanāṃśa |
|---|---|---|
| Originator | none identified (anonymous) | a **named individual** (N.C. Lahiri) |
| Establishment | emergent adaptation (Greek model) | an **individual + a government committee** |
| Era | ancient (~2nd c. BCE attestation) | modern (1952–57) |
| Authority | none presently identified | an identifiable **official standard** |
| Provenance shape | **convention-shaped** | **authored / standard-shaped** |

So the doctrine-vs-convention working model **discriminates across domains** —
it is not a gematria idiosyncrasy. Gematria's value table tops out in a *dated
convention*; the Lahiri ayanāṃśa tops out in an *establishing authority* (a
person and a governmental standard). That is the strongest available evidence
that the distinction is real and cross-domain — and it earns its keep by giving
different answers, not the same one.

## Consequence for the framework (observed, not encoded)

For the Vedic ayanāṃśa, the "deriving authority" slot the gematria table left
**empty** is here **filled**: an official Government of India standard. So the
Vedic SOURCE-A acquisition target is concrete and normative-tier —
the **Calendar Reform Committee Report (1955)** and/or the **Rāṣṭrīya Pañcāṅga**
— rather than an ancient text or an anonymous convention. Unlike gematria's
value table (a convention with no authority), the ayanāṃśa choice **has** an
authority, and it is a modern governmental one.

Per standing guidance, the provenance graph is **not** encoded — this is a
second data point, observed. Two more traditions of either shape would be
needed before the distinction is more than a working model.
